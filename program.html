1. Viriboni (Bhairavi Ata Thala Varnam)

Revered across generations as the "King of all Varnams" (Varnangalil Raja), "Viriboni" is the supreme benchmark of vocal discipline, breath control, and rhythmic command in the Carnatic pantheon.

Composed in the majestic Bhashanga ragam Bhairavi and set to the intricate 14-beat Ata talam, this piece traverses all three octaves. Sahana renders both the Purvanga (Pallavi, Anupallavi, Mukthayi Swaram) and Uttaranga (Charanam and four expansive Chittaswara suites) in two speeds (Prathama and Dwitiya Kalam), setting an authoritative, classical tone for the debut concert.

Ragam: Bhairavi (20th Melakarta Janya - Bhashanga)
Talam: Khanda Jathi Ata Talam (14 Beats)
Composer: Pacchimiriam Adiyappa
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

2. Gajavadana Beduve

A sparkling invocatory Devaranama in Kannada by the Father of Carnatic Music, Saint Purandara Dasa, offering heartfelt salutations to the elephant-faced Lord Ganesha to remove obstacles and bless the auspicious graduation debut.

Set to the effervescent pentatonic Ragam Hamsadhwani ("the voice of the swan"), this piece overflows with joyous energy. Sahana renders dynamic Kalpanaswarams (spontaneous swara permutations) highlighting the symmetry of the notes and engaging in a spirited rhythmic exchange with the violin and mridangam.

Ragam: Hamsadhwani (29th Melakarta Janya)
Talam: Adi Talam
Composer: Saint Purandara Dasa
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

3. Jnanamosagarada (Sub-Main)

The Sub-Main centerpiece of the first half. A profound philosophical masterpiece by Saint Tyagaraja beseeching Lord Rama: "Will You not bestow upon me supreme wisdom (Jnana) and the path of divine truth?"

Poorvikalyani is renowned for its intense emotional depth (gambhiram) and prathi madhyama curves. Sahana presents a structured Raga Alapana delineating the hallmark phrases of Poorvikalyani, followed by the kriti with brisk Neraval at "Paramathmudu" and multi-speed kalpanaswaras concluding in a synchronized makutam.

Ragam: Poorvikalyani (53rd Melakarta Janya)
Talam: Rupakam (3 Beats / 6 Matras)
Composer: Saint Tyagaraja
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

4. Ananda Natana Prakasam

One of the monumental Pancha Bhoota Sthala Kritis representing the Element of Ether/Space (Akasa Lingam) at the sacred temple of Chidambaram.

Composed in majestic Sanskrit by Muthuswami Dikshitar, this grand kriti vividly describes Lord Shiva's cosmic dance of bliss (Ananda Tandavam) in the Golden Hall of Chidambaram. The stately vakra phrases of Ragam Kedaram paired with the rhythmic 7-beat Misra Chapu talam evoke cosmic majesty and deep spiritual wonder.

Ragam: Kedaram (29th Melakarta Janya)
Talam: Misra Chapu (7 Beats / 3+4)
Composer: Muthuswami Dikshitar
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

5. Bhogendra Sayinam

A buoyant, melodious Sanskrit composition by the royal composer Maharaja Swathi Thirunal extolling Lord Vishnu reclining peacefully upon the celestial serpent king, Adisesha.

The unique scalar contours of Ragam Kuntalavarali (omitting Rishabham and Gandharam) combined with the lively 5-beat Khanda Chapu cycle create an undulating, wavy rhythm that mirrors the gentle sway of the ocean of milk (Ksheerasagara). Features brisk cascading chittaswaras.

Ragam: Kuntalavarali (28th Melakarta Janya)
Talam: Khanda Chapu (5 Beats)
Composer: Maharaja Swathi Thirunal
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

6. Sarojadala Netri (Grand Main Centerpiece)

The crowning zenith and monumental centerpiece of the concert. Syama Sastri's "Sarojadala Netri" is one of the grandest gems in Carnatic music, praising the lotus-eyed Goddess Meenakshi with unmatched rhythmic architecture and lyrical majesty.

This centerpiece is presented through elaborate classical Manodharma:
1. Grand Raga Alapana: An expansive, multi-octave exploration of Shankarabharanam across Mandra, Madhya, and Tara sthayis.
2. Tanam: Majestic rhythmic vocalization simulating the pluck of the Veena.
3. Kriti & Neraval: Deep thematic improvisation at "Sama Gana Vinodini" exploring Goddess Meenakshi as the lover of celestial music.
4. Kalpanaswaras: Mathematically rich swara korvais in 2-kalai Adi talam.
5. Tani Avartanam: An exhilarating percussion duel by Chi. Anirudh Suresh on Mridangam.

Ragam: Shankarabharanam (29th Melakarta)
Talam: Adi Talam (2-Kalai - 16 Beats)
Composer: Syama Sastri (Carnatic Trinity)
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar & Ensemble Solo

7. Abhimanam Vennamu (ArabhiManam)

A vibrant, uplifting piece pleading for the Lord's unconditional grace and affection: "Why have You withheld Your affection (Abhimanam) from this devoted servant?"

Ragam Arabhi is characterized by exhilarating, bright, swift phrases that evoke courage, enthusiasm (veera), and devotion. The piece features sparkling chittaswaras that sustain a celebratory tempo following the grand centerpiece.

Ragam: Arabhi / Begada
Talam: Adi Talam
Composer: Patnam Subramanya Iyer / Saint Tyagaraja
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

8. Andru Ivulagam (Thiruppavai - Sindhu Bhairavi)

The celebrated 24th Pasuram of Andal's sacred Tamil Thiruppavai, singing praises of Lord Vishnu's cosmic footsteps measuring the entire universe (Trivikrama Avatara): "Praise be to Thy feet that measured this whole world of yore!"

Rendered in the deeply emotional and universal raga Sindhu Bhairavi, this piece captures Andal's sublime surrender and unconditional bhakti. Sahana renders this with soulful Tamil diction and tender microtonal curves.

Ragam: Sindhu Bhairavi
Talam: Adi Talam
Composer: Kotai Nachiyar (Sri Andal)
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

9. Valli Kanavan Perai (Kavadi Chindu)

The infectious, pulsating folk-classical Kavadi Chindu dedicated to Lord Muruga: "Chant the name of Valli's consort, oh parrot, and sing His eternal glories!"

Characterized by lilting syncopated rhythms (Tisra Nadai) and joyful, catchy refrains, this piece showcases Sahana's versatility in shifting seamlessly from heavy classical manodharma to soulful, spirited regional folk traditions.

Ragam: Chenchurutti / Folk Ragamalika
Talam: Tisra Nadai / Adi
Composer: Traditional / Kavi Kunjara Bharati
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar

10. Maand Thillana

The rhythmic crescendo and aesthetic climax of the concert. Composed by the legendary violin maestro Lalgudi G. Jayaraman in the lilting, sweet Ragam Mand (Maand).

Driven by crisp rhythmic sollukattus (percussive syllables like "Ta Dhim Ta Na"), brisk swara passages, and playful cross-rhythmic syncopations, the vocalist, violinist Kum. Aishwarya Anand, and mridangist Chi. Anirudh Suresh lock into a thrilling, synchronized finale that fills the auditorium with celebratory ecstasy.

Ragam: Mand (Maand)
Talam: Adi Talam
Composer: Lalgudi G. Jayaraman
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar & Ensemble

11. Harivarasanam & Pavamana Mangalam

The sacred conclusion of the concert. It begins with "Harivarasanam", the celestial lullaby sung every night at the Sabarimala sanctum, and concludes with the auspicious Tyagaraja Mangalam "Pavamana".

Rendered in the supreme peace of Ragam Madhyamavathi—which according to tradition dissolves any minor tonal imbalances and leaves only harmony—Sahana, Kum. Aishwarya Anand, and Chi. Anirudh Suresh offer their gratitude to the Gurus, ancestors, musicians, and rasikas, praying for universal health, peace, and prosperity for all.

Ragam: Madhyamavathi & Saurashtram
Talam: Adi Talam
Composer: Kumbakudi Kulathur Iyer & Saint Tyagaraja
Performer: Kum. Sahana Kumar & Ensemble
